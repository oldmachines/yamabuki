# Whole-repository audit, September 2026

A pass over the entire tree — core, chips, cartridge and patch layer,
frontends, tests, build, CI, docs — asking four questions: is it documented,
is it easy to read and change, is it tested, and where does performance or
reliability leak. This file records what was done, what was measured, and
the ranked list of what is still worth doing. Every change shipped in the
same pull request as this document; every claim below was verified by the
gates in [`TESTING.md`](TESTING.md) before it was committed.

### Tests

- 43 new unit tests in the core where coverage was thinnest: CPU decimal
  arithmetic, block moves, page-cross and branch penalties, TSB/TRB; SPC700
  DIV/MUL/DAA/DAS/TCALL/SET1; HiROM and small-SRAM mapping; CPU I/O open-bus
  blends; the multiply/divide unit's documented quirk; WRAM port wrap; DMA
  count 0, decrement mode, pattern wrap, A-bus guards, HDMA repeat and
  indirect loads; timing constants; a serialize truncation property test;
  VRAM remap modes and the read-port side-effect rule; the opcode length
  table over all 256 × 4 width combinations; S-DD1 bank slices; header
  tie-breaking; registry key length.
- 13 new frontend and cart tests around the defects above.
- `takes.zig` joined the SDL test root; a `windows-latest` CI job runs the
  unit tests natively; CI cancels superseded runs and asserts the pinned
  Zig version instead of hardcoding it in eight places.

### Structure (pure code motion, verified byte-identical)

- `cart/sa1gen.zig` (13,513 lines) is now a 6,970-line root plus seven
  modules under `cart/sa1gen/`: `split` (the S5 mainline split), `offload`
  (S3b routine offload and the byte emitters), `window` (window offloads),
  `thunks` (padding allocators and every thunk body), `demirror`,
  `supermetroid` (the game-specific rebanks and their tests), `coverage`
  (static coverage extension). Every moved declaration is re-exported from
  the root, so `core.sa1gen.NAME` still resolves everywhere.
- `frontends/headless/main.zig` (6,433 lines) is now a 900-line root plus
  seven modules under `headless/cli/`: `args`, `dump`, `replay`, `cache`,
  `verify`, `generate`, `report`.
- Verification of both moves: the unit tests (the 54 sa1gen tests travelled
  with their subjects), every gate, and a regeneration of the shipped
  Super Metroid v78 patch with the split binary, compared byte for byte
  against `tests/surfaces/sm-sa1/sm-sa1-v78.bps` (see below).

### Performance: what was tried, what was measured, what was kept

Three small hot-path changes were made, all bit-identical under every
gate (102 goldens on both cores, 14 commercial boots, the deterministic
perf gate, fuzz, the v78 regeneration), and then timed against a
ReleaseFast build of the commit before them, interleaved, best of five,
on the development machine (Windows 11, x86-64):

| ROM (frames) | Baseline | With the changes |
|---|---|---|
| Super Mario World, 2,400 | 6.87 s | 6.93 s |
| Super Metroid, 2,400 | 3.75 s | 3.78 s |
| Kirby Super Star (SA-1), 2,400 | 6.28 s | 6.30 s |
| 8x8BGMapTileFlip, 3,000 | 2.27 s | 2.45 s |
| Rings (sprites), 3,000 | 5.02 s | 5.85 s |
| RotZoom (Mode 7), 3,000 | 2.09 s | 2.16 s |

- **Reverted:** replacing the renderer's three per-pixel divisions by a
  runtime tile size (always 8 or 16) with shifts and masks. It reads as an
  obvious win and made the bench ROMs 4-16% *slower*: the divisions by a
  two-valued `u16` were already being specialised by the optimiser, and
  the explicit shift amounts defeated that. The lesson is recorded here so
  nobody re-applies it without the timing.
- **Kept, no measurable change on the games above:** the SA-1 H/V timer is
  advanced arithmetically when both timer enables are off instead of
  running the chunked trigger loop after every SA-1 instruction
  (`chips/sa1.zig`); a Super FX cache-line fill classifies the program
  bank once instead of sixteen times (`chips/gsu.zig`); the rewind ring's
  state XOR runs 32 bytes at a time (`sdl/rewind.zig`, player only). They
  remove work and are verified; they are not the bottleneck.
- The two code-motion splits are exactly performance-neutral: a build with
  only the splits timed identically to the baseline on every ROM.

The `bench-check` FPS figures printed during the session varied by up to
40% between runs of the same binary; only the interleaved best-of-five
comparison above means anything, and CI's deterministic counters are the
gate for a reason.

Super Metroid v78 regeneration with the refactored binary (warm caches,
8.4 minutes): BEHAVIORALLY EQUIVALENT, patch byte-identical to
`tests/surfaces/sm-sa1/sm-sa1-v78.bps`.

## What was measured and left alone

- `apu.catchUp`'s two 64-bit divisions are by a comptime constant; LLVM
  already strength-reduces them.
- The 65816 `adcVal`/`sbcVal` pair is 80 near-duplicate lines. Folding them
  is safe only under the full SingleStepTests matrix; it is cosmetic and was
  left.
- The opcode metadata table exists exactly once (`usage_map.zig`); the
  suspicion that `sa1gen`, `patchgen` and `profile` carried copies was
  wrong.

## Still worth doing, ranked

1. **Move the `dbg_*` diagnostics behind a comptime core config.**
   `cpu/wdc65816.zig` tests five process-global flags per instruction and
   seven per write; `bus.cpuCycles` branches on `overclock` per access; the
   MMIO writer set pins an allocator into `Bus`. They are all SA-1 tooling
   that is off in play. A `CoreConfig.diagnostics` bool, instantiated true
   only by the headless runner, removes every one of them from the shipped
   core and also fixes the multi-console coupling (the fuzz shadow console
   and rewind share `lap_pending` and `dbg_watch_armed` today).
2. **Make the compositor's layer order comptime.** `resolvePixel` walks a
   runtime slice of up to twelve entries per pixel; there are six possible
   orders, all constants. Selecting them with an `inline` switch collapses
   the walk to predicated selects.
3. **Extract `trackPtrBanks` (about 500 lines of SA-1 pointer-bank
   heuristics) out of `console.zig`'s scheduler**, and the save-state code
   into its own file. Both are mechanical.
4. **Decompose `convertWholeGame` (2,275 lines) and `runSa1Gen` (1,231
   lines).** The natural seams are the two scan passes and the phase
   comments; the locals become a context struct. Not pure motion, so a
   separate, separately verified change.
5. **One ROM-loading helper for the three frontends.** Three copies with
   three size limits and two of them strip the copier header.
6. **The SDL session loop** (`app.zig`'s `run`, 1,266 lines): the ~40
   locals describing "the take on the machine" are one struct; the takes
   picker block, the shader-chain management and the overlay composition
   are each a file.
7. **Accurate core: hoist the per-span line buffers** out of `renderSpan`
   (about 11 KiB of stack per call, called per mid-line register write).
8. **A per-poll take through an item-pickup message box** for the SA-1
   verification corpus: no recording shows one, and the two crashes fixed
   in v76 and v78 were in exactly that code.
9. **A LICENSE file.** The project's own licence is unstated while every
   upstream's is documented.
10. **Small ones:** name the OAM/VRAM/H-blank magic numbers; `?u24` for the
    "no data access" sentinel; rename `Ppu.frame()` vs `Console.frame`;
    `Rewind.deinit`; debounce `config.save` on shader cycling; a fuzz seed
    sweep in a nightly job; `retro_cheat_set` is a no-op while
    `cheat.zig` already parses the codes RetroArch passes.
