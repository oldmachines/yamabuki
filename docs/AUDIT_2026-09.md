# Whole-repository audit, September 2026

A pass over the entire tree — core, chips, cartridge and patch layer,
frontends, tests, build, CI, docs — asking four questions: is it documented,
is it easy to read and change, is it tested, and where does performance or
reliability leak. This file records what was done, what was measured, and
the ranked list of what is still worth doing. Every change shipped in the
same pull request as this document; every claim below was verified by the
gates in [`TESTING.md`](TESTING.md) before it was committed.

## What changed

### Performance: what was tried, what was measured, what was kept

Three small hot-path changes were made, all bit-identical under every
gate (102 goldens on both cores, 14 commercial boots, the deterministic
perf gate, fuzz, the v78 regeneration), and then timed against a
ReleaseFast build of the commit before them, interleaved, best of five,
on the development machine (Windows 11, x86-64):

| ROM (frames) | Baseline | With the changes |
|---|---|---|
| Super Mario World, 2,400 | 6.87 s | 6.93 s |
| Super Metroid, 2,400 | 3.75 s | 3.78 s |
| Kirby Super Star (SA-1), 2,400 | 6.28 s | 6.30 s |
| 8x8BGMapTileFlip, 3,000 | 2.27 s | 2.45 s |
| Rings (sprites), 3,000 | 5.02 s | 5.85 s |
| RotZoom (Mode 7), 3,000 | 2.09 s | 2.16 s |

- **Reverted:** replacing the renderer's three per-pixel divisions by a
  runtime tile size (always 8 or 16) with shifts and masks. It reads as an
  obvious win and made the bench ROMs 4-16% *slower*: the divisions by a
  two-valued `u16` were already being specialised by the optimiser, and
  the explicit shift amounts defeated that. The lesson is recorded here so
  nobody re-applies it without the timing.
- **Kept, no measurable change on the games above:** the SA-1 H/V timer is
  advanced arithmetically when both timer enables are off instead of
  running the chunked trigger loop after every SA-1 instruction
  (`chips/sa1.zig`); a Super FX cache-line fill classifies the program
  bank once instead of sixteen times (`chips/gsu.zig`); the rewind ring's
  state XOR runs 32 bytes at a time (`sdl/rewind.zig`, player only). They
  remove work and are verified; they are not the bottleneck.
- The two code-motion splits are exactly performance-neutral: a build with
  only the splits timed identically to the baseline on every ROM.

The `bench-check` FPS figures printed during the session varied by up to
40% between runs of the same binary; only the interleaved best-of-five
comparison above means anything, and CI's deterministic counters are the
gate for a reason.

Super Metroid v78 regeneration with the refactored binary (warm caches,
8.4 minutes): BEHAVIORALLY EQUIVALENT, patch byte-identical to
`tests/surfaces/sm-sa1/sm-sa1-v78.bps`.

## The follow-up series (September 14)

The ranked list below was then worked through, one commit per item, each
with the full gates and an interleaved before/after timing (bench core,
ReleaseFast, best of five, and the headless accurate core). Results:

| Step | Change | Verified | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conversion diagnostics compiled out of the shipping core (`diagnostics` knob; headless and the core tests keep them) | all gates on both configurations; v78 byte-identical | SMW -3.6%, Kirby (SA-1) -3.9%, Rings -2.4%, accurate core -3.1% |
| 2 | Compositor instantiated per priority order, walk unrolled | goldens on both cores, bench-check, fuzz | SMW -20%, Super Metroid -18%, Rings -15%, RotZoom -19%, accurate core -23.5% |
| 3 | `trackPtrBanks` and the save-state container out of `console.zig` (2,230 → 1,558 lines) | all gates; libretro parity; v78 byte-identical twice | neutral (±1%) |
| 5 | One ROM reader and one patch-apply message table for every frontend | unit, libretro, commercial gate through a patch, SDL smoke | neutral |
| 6 | `app.zig` split into the session root and six modules (3,106 → 1,652 lines) | 547 unit tests, SDL smoke on both video paths, shot path | neutral |
| 7 | Accurate-core span fill cache (reuse the layer fill across mid-line register writes) | bit-identical on every gate and on four commercial games | **slower**: accurate core +7%, Super Metroid +4%; reverted |
| 10 | Named the VRAM/OAM/H-blank/DMA-trace magic numbers; `Ppu.frame` → `frameRows`; `Rewind.deinit` (the player never freed its ring); config saves debounced on shader cycling (one write 1.5 s after the last tap, or at exit); libretro `retro_cheat_set` wired to the cheat parser; a nightly fuzz seed sweep | unit tests, goldens on both cores, libretro, fuzz, SDL smoke | neutral |

Step 7's lesson: the benchmark scenes have few mid-line writes, so the
cache rarely hit, while its bookkeeping (a store on every PPU register
write, which HDMA-heavy games do every line) and its ten KiB of buffers
in the PPU struct cost on every frame. Moving the buffers to the struct's
tail did not change that. A span cache only pays on a scene that races
the beam dozens of times a line; none of the goldens or the four
commercial titles measured does, so it was dropped rather than shipped on
faith. Not attempted for the same reason: the `?u24` sentinel for the
last data access — an optional is eight bytes and two stores on the
hottest write in the core, for no functional gain.

## What was measured and left alone

- `apu.catchUp`'s two 64-bit divisions are by a comptime constant; LLVM
  already strength-reduces them.
- The 65816 `adcVal`/`sbcVal` pair is 80 near-duplicate lines. Folding them
  is safe only under the full SingleStepTests matrix; it is cosmetic and was
  left.
- The opcode metadata table exists exactly once (`usage_map.zig`); the
  suspicion that `sa1gen`, `patchgen` and `profile` carried copies was
  wrong.

## Still worth doing, ranked

Of the ten items ranked after the first pass, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 10 shipped
(table above), 7 was tried, measured slower and dropped, and 9 (a LICENSE
file) is the owner's decision. What remains:

1. **Decompose `convertWholeGame` (2,275 lines) and `runSa1Gen` (1,231
   lines).** The natural seams are the two scan passes and the phase
   comments; the locals become a context struct. Not pure motion, so a
   separate, separately verified change (the v78 regeneration is the
   gate, eight minutes on warm caches).
2. **A per-poll take through an item-pickup message box** for the SA-1
   verification corpus: no recording shows one, and the two crashes fixed
   in v76 and v78 were in exactly that code. Needs a person at the
   controller.
3. **The SDL session loop's forty locals** describing "the take on the
   machine" are still one implicit object inside `run` (1,270 lines);
   the module split took the helpers out, not the state.
4. **Accurate-core span rendering** is now the biggest measured cost
   that nothing addresses: a real scene that races the beam dozens of
   times a line is needed to design (and prove) a fill cache; the
   attempt on the goldens' scenes only added cost.
5. **A LICENSE file.**
